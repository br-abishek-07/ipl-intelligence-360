# Ten debated IPL questions — data-based answers

All calculations are from the user-supplied, Cricsheet-derived IPL 2008–2026 master tables unless noted. These are retrospective **descriptive** conclusions and are not causal or future-model claims. Team identity rebrand handling follows the application's code.

## 1. Was Virat Kohli a better IPL captain than MS Dhoni?

**Answer:** The available captain registry only verifies IPL FINAL captains: Dhoni led 5 championship-winning finals, Kohli 0. This supports a finals-trophy comparison, NOT a complete captaincy win-rate ranking. A full match-by-match captain registry is still required.

**Evidence:** 2008–2026 IPL finals captain registry. **Caveat:** Partial leadership evidence; not a definitive claim about all captaincy qualities.

## 2. Is Virat Kohli the better IPL batter than MS Dhoni?

**Answer:** Kohli scored 9,336 runs at 134.80 strike rate across 275 recorded batting matches; Dhoni scored 5,439 at 137.45 across 241. Kohli leads career run volume; Dhoni has the higher career strike rate in this source. Neither measure alone settles "better" because roles and batting positions differ.

**Evidence:** Cricsheet regulation batting_player_match (2008–2026). **Caveat:** Compare role, entry over and not-out frequency before making an overall player-value claim.

## 3. Has Mumbai Indians clearly surpassed Chennai Super Kings historically?

**Answer:** Both have 5 and 5 IPL titles, respectively. MI won 157 of 291 decided matches (54.0%), while CSK won 148 of 265 (55.8%). The title count is level; CSK has the better decided-match win rate.

**Evidence:** IPL Finals and franchise results, 2008–2026. **Caveat:** Different franchise match counts; results do not isolate squad strength.

## 4. Does CSK really dominate RCB head-to-head?

**Answer:** In 35 decided IPL meetings, CSK won 21 and RCB won 14. CSK leads this historical match-up; the margin may change under particular seasons or grounds.

**Evidence:** Cricsheet match-level results, 2008–2026. **Caveat:** All-time head-to-head record, not a future-match forecast.

## 5. Is CSK historically stronger than MI when they face each other?

**Answer:** In 41 decided IPL meetings, CSK won 20 and MI won 21. MI has the narrow head-to-head edge, despite CSK's slightly higher league-wide win rate.

**Evidence:** Cricsheet match-level results, 2008–2026. **Caveat:** Small head-to-head edge; past meetings cannot guarantee future results.

## 6. Does winning the toss mean you will win the match?

**Answer:** Toss-winning teams won 634 of 1234 decided matches (51.38%). That is only +1.38 percentage points above a 50% reference, so a toss win is not a strong stand-alone win predictor.

**Evidence:** One toss-winning team observation per decided IPL match. **Caveat:** Correlation only; toss choice, venue and teams may confound.

## 7. Is chasing generally more successful than defending in the IPL?

**Answer:** Chasing sides won 672 of 1234 decided IPL matches (54.46%). Batting-first sides won the remaining 562 (45.54%). Historical chasing advantage exists, but it varies by venue, target and season.

**Evidence:** Decided IPL matches; chase role taken from team_match_summary. **Caveat:** Raw outcomes, not adjustment for dew, opponent, pitch, toss or DLS.

## 8. Was IPL batting really more explosive in 2026 than in the inaugural 2008 season?

**Answer:** Among non-DLS first innings of at least 100 legal balls, 2008 averaged 165.47 runs (n=53) and 2026 averaged 197.50 (n=70), a difference of +32.03 runs.

**Evidence:** Filtered first-innings summary, 2008 and 2026. **Caveat:** Descriptive era change; not necessarily caused by any one rule change.

## 9. Is a 200-run first innings still an exceptional event?

**Answer:** In 2016, 4 of 56 eligible first innings reached 200+ (7.1%). In 2026, 37 of 70 did (52.9%). In the available sample, crossing 200 became far more common.

**Evidence:** Non-DLS first innings with >=100 legal balls, seasons 2016 and 2026. **Caveat:** Denominator is filtered; incomplete/short innings excluded.

## 10. Does winning the powerplay usually separate winning and losing teams?

**Answer:** For the 2022–2026 non-DLS decided-match sample, winners scored at 9.70 runs/over in the powerplay (n=358 batting innings), compared with 8.60 for losers (n=358). That is an association, not proof that simply increasing powerplay scoring causes victory.

**Evidence:** Team phase deliveries matched to final match outcomes, 2022–2026. **Caveat:** Descriptive association subject to chasing context and opponent difficulty.
